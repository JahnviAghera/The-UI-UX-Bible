How people see

01

Think before you push pixels

Principles first, tools second. Meet users' expectations, then innovate.

Five planes of every product Canon

Jesse James Garrett's model: every visual decision on the surface rests on the layers below it. When a screen feels wrong, find the lowest broken layer. A colour problem is sometimes really a scope problem.

"I spent way too much time trying to master tools before understanding basic design principles."Cecilia Kim Feed

Five mindset shifts Feed

  1. Learn colour, type and hierarchy before Figma shortcuts.
  2. Collect designs you love every day and write down why each one works.
  3. Make something small daily, even a single button or palette.
  4. Feedback is gold, not criticism. Show work early.
  5. Your own perspective is your edge. Stop copying trends.

Read in this order Feed

Start with Krug: it takes about two hours and pays off right away. Then read Norman for the psychology, and build from there.

"Meet those expectations first, then innovate. Don't reinvent the wheel when it works."Cecilia Kim, Mealmate redesign
Try it

Open three apps you use daily. For each, write one sentence about a decision on each plane, from strategy up to surface.

03

Perception & hierarchy

People group what's close and alike, and scan before they read. Design for both.

Gestalt grouping, drawn Canon

Proximity

Close items read as one group. Proximity beats colour and shape.

Similarity

Things that look alike seem to work alike. Keep every link one style.

Common region

A shared container (card, background) groups items more strongly than spacing alone.

Connectedness

A line between items (steppers, timelines) beats every other grouping cue.

Von Restorff

The one that differs gets remembered. Give the primary CTA that role, and only that.

Sources: NN/g, Proximity Principle in Visual Design · Laws of UX

People scan in an F Canon

On text-heavy pages, eyes sweep across the top, sweep a shorter line lower down, then drop down the left edge. Words on the right and further down get skipped. NN/g found this pattern on desktop and mobile alike.

Other scanning patterns

  • Layer-cake: reading headings only. Make headings carry the meaning.
  • Spotted: hunting for numbers, links or keywords.
  • Marking: eyes fixed in one spot while thumbs scroll (common on mobile).
  • Bypassing: skipping list items that start with the same words.

Source: NN/g, F-Shaped Pattern of Reading on the Web

Building hierarchy: six dials

SIZEBigger = firstUse a clear ratio between levels (e.g. 34 → 22 → 17 pt in iOS), not 16 vs 17.
WEIGHTFew weightsRegular + semibold covers most UIs. "Too many font weights = visual chaos." (Cecilia)
COLOURGrey de-emphasisesUse secondary text in muted grey, and save the accent for the one action.
SPACESpace separatesKeep a heading closer to its own text than to the section above it.
POSITIONTop-left winsPut the most important content where the F starts.
CONTAINERSCards groupUse common region sparingly. If everything is a card, nothing stands out.
Try it

Take a screenshot of any screen and blur it heavily, or squint at it. Whatever you still see first is your real hierarchy. Does it match what matters most?

Part II

Visual craft

04

When it feels off, add space

Change padding, line height and section margins before you touch colour or fonts.

The three-dial fix Feed

This was Cecilia's biggest mistake in her first freelance year. She swapped colours and fonts for hours when the real problem was that everything sat too close together. Keep the design, font, colour and content the same, and change only:

  • Padding around each element
  • Line height in the text
  • Margin between sections
"White space is not wasted space. It gives your eye a place to rest."Cecilia Kim

Cleaner screens, quieter details Feed

  • Use 4px steps on mobile (Cecilia: "makes developer handoff seamless"), or 8px steps. Pick one and stick to it.
  • Swap pure black for dark grey: it's warmer and less harsh.
  • Set dividers to 10% opacity, or delete them and let images separate rows.
  • Don't centre everything. Align to a layout grid.
  • Bigger images need bigger titles, or proportions break.

Internal ≤ external Canon

Proximity in practice: the space inside a group must be smaller than the space between groups. NN/g's example is the form label. A label that sits halfway between two fields belongs to neither.

Source: NN/g, Proximity Principle

Try it

Screenshot one of your own screens. Redline every gap in Figma. Snap each to 4/8/16/24/32, then double the section gaps. Compare the two side by side.

05

Type works through contrast

Use two fonts at most: serif for headings, sans for body. Then set a scale and a line length.

Why pairs fail Feed

When two fonts are too similar, the eye doesn't know where to look first. There's no hierarchy, so the design feels flat even when the layout is solid.

Your weekly menu
Seven dinners planned from the recipes you saved. Swap any day with one tap.

Line length: 50–75 characters Canon

Baymard recommends 50–75 characters per line. Long lines make it hard to find the start of the next one. Short lines break the reading rhythm and make people skip words. WCAG's AAA criterion 1.4.8 caps lines at 80 characters.

Source: Baymard, Readability: The Optimal Line Length

Cecilia's rule for spacing applies to text too. When a paragraph feels hard to read, the fix is often not a new font but a better measure and more line height. Readers track back to the start of each line, and a very long line makes that jump unreliable, while a very short one interrupts the rhythm every few words.

Text on photos Feed

Add a black gradient that fades upward, so white text stays readable on any image. Many food apps on Mobbin do this.

Train your eye: type.method.ac (kerning) · shape.method.ac (letterforms)

Colour, light & dark

Use a 60-30-10 split, check contrast, choose psychology on purpose, and design the dark theme properly.

60 · 30 · 10 Feed

60% is a dominant neutral, 30% a supporting colour, and 10% an accent you save for actions and highlights.

60 · neutral
30
10
  • Complementary (opposites, e.g. blue + orange) adds energy.
  • Analogous (neighbours, e.g. blue, green, teal) feels calm.
  • Test colours in context: a colour can look perfect alone and wrong next to others.

Psychology, chosen on purpose Feed

Bluetrust
Greenfresh, growth
Redurgency, appetite
Mealmate liked green, but chose red: "We didn't want to reinvent the psychology."Cecilia Kim, branding sprint

Never let colour carry meaning alone. Pair it with an icon, a label or a shape so colour-blind users get the same message.

Dark theme is not inverted light Canon

  • Use a dark grey surface (#121212), not pure black.
  • Show elevation with lighter surfaces. Material adds a white overlay that grows stronger as a surface gets higher.
  • Use off-white text at 87% / 60% / 38% opacity for high, medium and disabled emphasis.
  • Use lighter, less saturated accents: saturated colours "vibrate" on dark backgrounds.

Source: Google, Design a dark theme with Material

Try it

Build your palette as 5 tokens (bg, surface, text, muted, accent) in both themes. Run every text pair through the checker above. Fix any failure before you think about anything else.

Part III

Interaction

07

Thumbs & targets

Put the main action where the thumb rests, and make every target big enough to hit.

Fitts's law Feed

The time to reach a target depends on its distance and size. Closer and bigger means faster. Most people hold their phone in one hand.

"Users can now actually tap it without having to adjust their grip."Cecilia Kim, redesign part 7

Target sizes Canon

Aim for 44–48 for anything tappable. That satisfies Apple, Material and WCAG AAA at once. WCAG 2.2's AA floor of 24×24 CSS px is the legal minimum, not the goal.

Sources: W3C, What's new in WCAG 2.2 · Front-End Checklist, touch targets

Mobile isn't a shrunken website Feed

  • Start at 1:1 parity with web, then add what only a phone can do (Ellie got a voice button).
  • Widgets fight the real competitor, which is being forgotten: "present without being too pushy".
08

States, feedback, loading

Every element has more than one state, and every wait needs the right signal.

One button, seven states Canon

Designers who draw only the default state leave developers to invent the rest. Draw all of them as component variants.

DefaultSave menu
HoverSave menu
PressedSave menu
FocusSave menu
DisabledSave menu
LoadingSaving…
Success✓ Saved

This is heuristic #1, visibility of system status, applied to the smallest thing on screen. The focus ring is required for keyboard users (WCAG).

The three response-time limits Canon

0.1 sFeels instant. No indicator needed.
1 sNoticeable, but thought stays unbroken.
10 sAttention is lost. Show progress and a way to cancel.

The Doherty threshold puts the productive sweet spot at under 400 ms, where neither the person nor the system waits on the other.

Sources: NN/g, Response Times: The 3 Important Limits · Laws of UX

Empty states do three jobs Canon

  1. Say what's happening. Is it empty, loading, or failed? Never show "No items" while data is still loading.
  2. Teach. Explain what fills this space and how.
  3. Offer a path. Give a direct button to the first task, plus a low-risk option such as sample data.

Source: NN/g, Designing Empty States in Complex Applications

Try it

Pick one screen in your app and list every state it can be in: first use, loading, partial, full, error, offline. Design the three you've never drawn.

09

Forms & errors

Keep labels visible, write specific errors, and never throw away what someone typed.

Placeholders are not labels Canon

NN/g lists the costs of using placeholder text as a label:

  • The hint vanishes when you type, so people forget what the field needs.
  • Users can't check their entries before submitting.
  • Light-grey text usually fails contrast, and some screen readers skip it.
  • It can look like a filled-in value, so empty fields get missed.

Use a visible label above the field. Put hints and format examples below the label, outside the field. Floating labels are a compromise, not a fix.

Source: NN/g, Placeholders in Form Fields Are Harmful

Writing error messages Canon

✗An error occurred. (Code 400)
✗Invalid input!
✓That email is missing an "@". Try name@example.com.
  • Visible: next to the field, using colour and an icon or text, with severity matching the problem.
  • Clear: plain language, specific, with the fix included, and never blaming the user.
  • Efficient: keep the input, suggest corrections, and prevent the error in the first place.

Source: NN/g, Error-Message Guidelines

More form rules

  • Chunk long forms into logical groups (personal, address, payment). 3 × 4 fields feels lighter than 12 fields in one block (NN/g).
  • Don't ask twice: WCAG 2.2's "Redundant Entry" says data a user already entered should be auto-filled or selectable.
  • Use the right keyboard (inputmode) and autocomplete on every field.
  • Make the primary action describe the result: "Create account", not "Submit".
10

Motion

Motion explains change. Keep it fast, eased and purposeful, and make it optional.

How long? Canon

ContextDuration
Mobile, typical transition~300 ms
Element entering screen225 ms
Element leaving screen195 ms
Large full-screen changeup to 375 ms
Desktop150–200 ms
Feels slow> 400 ms

Source: Material Design, Duration & easing

Rules

  • Animate one thing at a time when you can, and stagger groups by 20–50 ms.
  • Never block input while a decorative animation plays.
  • Respect reduce motion: swap movement for a fade, or nothing. Every figure in this book does this.
  • WCAG 2.2: anything done by dragging must also work with a single tap or click.
  • For inspiration, study 60fps and Ripplix (micro-interactions).

Design for the widest range of people from the first wireframe. It improves things for everyone.

Process & testing

Separate structure from surface, and test with five people before you polish.

Case study: Mealmate, one screen with a 1-hour timebox Feed

Cecilia picked the weekly menu: the simplest screen to redesign, with the most impact. Scroll through the four states.

Why five users? Canon

For qualitative testing (finding problems), the Nielsen and Landauer model says 5 participants find about 85% of usability issues. After that, each new person mostly repeats what you've seen. Fix the problems and test again with another five.

For quantitative studies (measuring time, success rate or comparing designs), you need 30+ participants, and 40 or more for narrow confidence intervals.

Source: NN/g, How Many Participants for a UX Interview / Test?

Running a 30-minute test

  1. Write 3–5 realistic tasks that don't give the answer away ("Plan dinners for this week", not "Tap Generate").
  2. Ask the person to think aloud. Stay quiet and don't help. Note where they hesitate.
  3. Log each issue with a severity: blocker, major or minor.
  4. Fix the blockers, then test again with the next five people.
Try it

Test your attendance app with 5 classmates this week. One task each, 10 minutes per person. Count how many hit the same problem.

Figma & design systems

Frames, auto layout, components, styles and tokens. Change once, update everywhere.

Think in stacks Feed

Cecilia brings this from iOS: Figma's auto layout works like SwiftUI's horizontal and vertical stacks. If you build screens in Flutter, it's the same idea as Row and Column. Break every screen into these and it adapts to every phone size.

  • Frames, not groups: frames carry layout rules; groups don't.
  • Auto layout everywhere: no more broken layouts.
  • Layout grids: look up the web and mobile standards.

A file developers love Feed

  • Thumbnail, plus a start page linking the PRD and research
  • Problem statement and competitor analysis
  • Design intentions and "How might we" statements
  • Iterations grouped by milestone
  • Finals: before/after, notes on changes, marked for Dev Mode

via UI Gradient (@ux.edward)

Tokens in three tiers Canon

A token is a named design decision. Name tokens by role, not value, and a rebrand becomes a one-line change. This is what UI Gradient's design.md files describe for AI agents.

1 · PrimitiveThe raw scalered-600 = #D9393Espace-4 = 16pxEvery available value. Never used directly in components.
2 · SemanticThe rolecolor-action-primary-bg → red-600space-section → space-6What a value is for. Light and dark themes swap values at this layer.
3 · ComponentThe exceptionbtn-primary-hover-bg → red-700Only where a component needs its own decision.

Naming pattern (namespace · object · base · modifier) from Singapore Government Design System, Token naming. In Figma these are Variables, with modes for light and dark.

Personality & building with AI

Handmade detail makes it memorable. Taste is what steers the AI.

Spend personality where people linger Feed

  • Onboarding: the part everyone taps through. Amy's illustrations loop through 3 frames.
  • Rewards: hand-drawn cat-meme streak badges instead of generic trophies.
  • Mascots: Lily's ghost, Ellie's hamster.

Portfolio & career

"Clients don't hire pretty pictures, they hire thought processes."

Cut, don't add Feed

Keep only projects where you can walk someone through every decision, iteration and reason. Hiring managers ask one thing: does this person think well?

"If your portfolio feels like it's missing something, try removing things instead of adding more."Cecilia Kim

  • Post your process 2–3× a week, like a design journal.
  • Passion projects matter more in the AI era (Jhanvi).
  • Tool: no-code you know for speed, code for something unique, or a template you customise for both.
